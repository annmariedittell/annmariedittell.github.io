Case Study

Designing Instructions for Screen Reader Users

Preventing an accessibility barrier during design, before it reached development.

My Role
Accessibility Specialist
Focus
Screen reader UX
Stage
Design review
Collaboration
UX designer

Challenge

During a review of a Figma design for an application screen, I noticed that instructions telling users to read the scenario and select all that apply were positioned at the bottom of the screen.

For a screen reader user, that meant the interactive content could be encountered before the instructions explaining what to do.

My Role

I reviewed the design for accessibility before it was handed off to development and worked directly with the UX designer to explain the experience from a screen reader user’s perspective.

Solution

I recommended moving the instructions to the top of the experience, before the scenario and interactive response options. Once the user impact was clear, the UX designer accepted the recommendation and updated the design before development began.

Outcome

  • Reduced downstream accessibility rework
  • Avoided redesign and development changes later
  • Improved the experience for screen reader users
  • Supported a faster accessibility review later in the lifecycle
Key takeaway: Accessibility is most effective when it is incorporated into design decisions early, rather than treated as a final compliance check.
