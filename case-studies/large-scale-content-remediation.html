Higher Education Accessibility Case Study

Leading Large-Scale Accessible Content Remediation

Leading a one-year accessibility initiative across 200+ higher education learning supplements while building a repeatable remediation model for future projects.

My Role
Accessibility and Compliance Manager
Duration
One year
Scope
200+ learning supplements across Math, Science, Humanities, and Career Ready
Collaboration
10 internal teams, 2 remediation vendors, and Accessibility QA

Challenge

A large portfolio of higher education digital learning supplements required accessibility remediation to meet WCAG 2.1 and Section 508 requirements.

The year-long initiative included more than 200 supplements spanning Math, Science, Humanities, and Career Ready content. The work involved 10 internal teams: four within Math, three within Science, one within Humanities, and two within Career Ready.

The size of the portfolio was only part of the challenge. Different disciplines and supplement types presented different accessibility considerations, particularly Math and Science content that needed to be rendered appropriately so screen reader users could access and understand the material.

With multiple internal teams, two external remediation vendors, and an internal Accessibility QA team involved, the program needed a consistent process for prioritization, remediation, communication, validation, and progress tracking.

My Role

As Accessibility and Compliance Manager, I led the accessibility remediation initiative across the higher education portfolio.

I coordinated work among the internal Math, Science, Humanities, and Career Ready teams while also working directly with two of our core remediation vendors responsible for remediating the supplements.

My role included establishing accessibility expectations, coordinating remediation priorities, addressing questions and risks, monitoring progress, and ensuring that completed work moved through accessibility quality assurance before being considered finished.

Rather than treating the initiative as hundreds of unrelated files, I managed it as a coordinated accessibility program with a common workflow, shared quality expectations, and clear accountability across teams and vendors.

Creating a Scalable Remediation Workflow

A project of this size required a repeatable process.

We established a workflow that moved content from identification and remediation through accessibility validation, with project activity tracked in Jira and Confluence.

This gave teams visibility into:

  • Remediation status and priorities
  • Vendor assignments and progress
  • Accessibility questions and issues
  • QA validation
  • Outstanding corrections
  • Overall project progress

The structure helped keep 10 internal teams, two external vendors, and Accessibility QA aligned throughout the one-year initiative.

Addressing Math and Science Accessibility

Math and Science content required additional attention because accessibility depended on more than the visual presentation of the material.

We worked to ensure that mathematical and scientific content was rendered correctly for the specific type of supplement so that screen reader users could access the information appropriately.

This meant considering how the content would actually be interpreted and navigated with assistive technology, rather than relying solely on whether it appeared visually correct.

Those considerations were incorporated into the remediation and validation process so specialized content received the same level of accessibility quality as the rest of the portfolio.

Building Quality Into the Process

Remediation was not considered complete when a vendor returned an updated supplement.

After remediation, our internal Accessibility QA team validated the changes to confirm that identified accessibility issues had been addressed and that the content worked appropriately with assistive technology.

If issues remained, the content moved back through the remediation process before final approval.

The validation step was especially important at this scale because it provided consistent quality expectations across different disciplines, teams, supplement types, and vendors.

Looking Beyond the Immediate Project

As the initiative progressed, the workflow became more than a process for completing one remediation effort.

We were developing a repeatable model for organizing large accessibility remediation projects, coordinating internal teams and external vendors, tracking work, validating quality, and maintaining accountability.

The lessons learned during the project helped establish a stronger approach for future accessibility initiatives rather than requiring each new remediation project to develop its own process from scratch.

Outcome

Over the course of one year, the initiative coordinated accessibility remediation for more than 200 higher education digital learning supplements across Math, Science, Humanities, and Career Ready.

The effort brought together 10 internal teams, two remediation vendors, an internal Accessibility QA team, and centralized Jira and Confluence tracking.

The project achieved its immediate goal of improving accessibility and supporting WCAG 2.1 and Section 508 conformance, but its longer-term impact was equally important.

The workflow developed during the initiative became the model used for subsequent remediation projects, providing a repeatable structure for vendor coordination, internal collaboration, accessibility QA, progress tracking, and final validation.

Key takeaway: Large-scale accessibility remediation requires more than fixing content. By coordinating 10 internal teams, two remediation vendors, and Accessibility QA across a 200+ asset portfolio, I helped establish a scalable remediation and validation model that the organization could reuse for future accessibility initiatives.
