Case Study

Designing an Accessible Public Information Site with Figma Make

Rapid prototyping, accessibility review, and stakeholder collaboration from concept to approved design.

My Role
Prompting, UX review, accessibility
Tools
Figma Make
Focus
Accessibility + usability
Outcome
Approved for development

Challenge

Users did not have a public-facing website where they could access general information. Much of the information was available only after logging into the system, and users without login access often had to submit a help ticket.

The goal was to support a more self-service model and make common information easier to find.

My Role

I helped translate the stakeholder’s vision into working design concepts using Figma Make. I wrote and refined prompts, reviewed layouts for accessibility and usability, simplified the experience, and iterated on the designs.

Accessibility and Usability

  • Logical heading structure
  • Clear page regions
  • Keyboard access for interactive elements
  • Appropriate ARIA recommendations where native HTML would not be enough
  • Simplified layouts and content flow

Exploring Multiple Directions

I created three distinct design concepts with different layouts and visual styles. During the client presentation, I walked through the strengths and tradeoffs of each option and tracked which elements the client liked or wanted changed.

That feedback became the foundation for the final design.

Outcome

Because of the speed of the AI-assisted prototyping process, I created the revised final design the following day. The client approved it and sent it to the development team for implementation.

  • Gave stakeholders concrete visual options to evaluate
  • Made it easier for the client to articulate preferences
  • Incorporated accessibility before development
  • Reduced time from initial concept to approved design
Key takeaway: AI-assisted prototyping is most valuable when speed is combined with accessibility review, usability thinking, stakeholder feedback, and human judgment.
