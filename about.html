About

Accessibility leadership starts with understanding the user.

I am an accessibility and inclusive UX leader who helps organizations turn accessibility requirements into practical strategies, stronger products, and sustainable ways of working.

Accessibility Leadership & Impact

My work sits at the intersection of accessibility strategy, inclusive UX, technical execution, and program leadership. I connect user needs to organizational priorities and help teams turn accessibility requirements into decisions and practices they can apply throughout delivery.

  • Managed a $2.7 million STEM accessibility program, coordinating roadmaps, vendors, risks, executive reporting, and cross-functional workstreams.
  • Directed remediation of more than 200 higher education digital learning supplements while strengthening the workflow used for future remediation projects.
  • Used accessibility defect analytics and team feedback to identify systemic issues, create reusable standards and guidance, and build a company-wide internal Accessibility Hub for project teams.
  • Built and governed a separate Accessibility Hub for internal teams, core vendors, and university audiences, defining how accessibility standards were applied across learning applications and content, standardizing remediation practices, and giving universities concrete evidence of accessibility during procurement.
  • Led an AI-assisted image-description initiative that generated approximately $1.9 million in savings over three years while improving accessibility and scalability.

How I Lead Accessibility

I believe accessibility is most effective when it is built into how an organization works, not added as a final compliance check. I partner across product, engineering, design, content, quality assurance, project management, vendors, and leadership to make accessibility understandable, actionable, and sustainable.

That means bringing accessibility into strategy and requirements early, helping teams understand user impact, identifying systemic barriers, building repeatable processes, and validating that solutions work in practice.

When I evaluate an accessibility issue, I look beyond the defect:
  • What is the user trying to accomplish?
  • What barrier are they experiencing?
  • Can they still complete the task?
  • How significant is the impact?
  • Is the issue isolated or systemic?
  • What can we change so the problem is less likely to recur?

Leadership Grounded in the Work

I stay close to the technical discipline. My experience includes WCAG and Section 508 testing, screen reader and keyboard evaluation, accessible UX reviews, Figma and Figma Make prototyping, component reviews, remediation guidance, accessible document development, and accessibility validation.

That hands-on foundation helps me communicate effectively with product, design, and engineering teams and keeps broader accessibility decisions grounded in the real experience of people using assistive technology.

Some of my most valuable work happens before formal testing begins, when design and development decisions can still be changed with less effort and greater impact.

Leadership Foundation

Before moving into digital accessibility, I spent more than 20 years in education and educational leadership. I began my career as a teacher, later founded and operated a K–8 charter school, and served as principal for 13 years. I also worked with local education agencies across Arizona through the Arizona Department of Education.

Those experiences continue to shape how I lead today. They strengthened my ability to explain complex concepts clearly, work across disciplines, manage people and programs, balance requirements with practical constraints, understand diverse user needs, and build processes that help teams succeed.

What Drives My Work

Accessibility is ultimately about giving people equitable access to information, services, and experiences. My goal is to help organizations make accessibility part of how they plan, design, build, and improve products so progress is sustainable and does not depend on one specialist catching problems at the end.
