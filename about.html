About

Accessibility starts with understanding the user.

I am an accessibility professional focused on creating digital experiences that are usable, understandable, and inclusive from the beginning.

From Education to Accessibility

Before moving into digital accessibility, I spent more than 20 years in education and educational leadership.

I began my career as a teacher and later founded and operated a K–8 charter school, serving as principal for 13 years. I also worked with local education agencies across Arizona through the Arizona Department of Education.

Those experiences shaped how I work today. They taught me to look at problems from the learner or user’s perspective, explain complex concepts in understandable language, collaborate across disciplines, balance requirements with practical solutions, and build processes that help teams succeed.

My Approach to Accessibility

I believe accessibility is most effective when it is part of the entire product lifecycle.

That means participating in early design discussions, reviewing prototypes before development, helping teams understand the impact of accessibility decisions, testing with assistive technology, identifying systemic issues, and validating fixes.

When I find an accessibility issue, I ask:
  • What is the user trying to accomplish?
  • What barrier are they experiencing?
  • Can they still complete the task?
  • How significant is the impact?
  • Is this isolated or systemic?
  • How can we prevent the problem from recurring?

Accessibility + UX

Some of my most valuable work happens before formal accessibility testing begins. I enjoy working with UX designers and stakeholders during the design and prototyping process, where relatively small changes can prevent larger accessibility problems later.

My experience includes accessible UX reviews, Figma and Figma Make prototyping, Section 508 and WCAG testing, screen reader and keyboard testing, remediation guidance, accessible document development, and accessibility validation.

What Drives My Work

Accessibility is ultimately about giving people equal access to information, services, and experiences. My goal is to help teams make accessibility a natural part of how they design and build products, rather than something that must be corrected at the end.
